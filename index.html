You got the money.
Now let's get the power.

A national union of people who have been through a guaranteed income pilot. We know what direct cash did. We should be the ones saying so.

The Basic Income Solidarity Community (BISC) Union is a community-led initiative run by people power.

We believe that lasting change comes from organized communities.

Our union, founded by former pilot participants, welcomes people from across the U.S. to co-design a community that supports and advocates for direct cash and its benefits. Our mission is to win a basic income for every person in America by turning curiosity into courage, solidarity into power, and isolation into belonging. We are reclaiming our collective right to economic security and instilling dignity in the process.

Who this is for

Right now we're starting with people who took part in a guaranteed income or basic income pilot — current or past. That's roughly 40,000 people across the country.

We're starting there, not stopping there. The union opens wider as we grow.

Why join?

Your experience is evidence

Pilots produced reports, studies, and headlines. The people who lived them got measured, but rarely got to say what any of it meant. Together we decide how that story gets told — and who tells it.

You are not the only one

Roughly 40,000 people across the country have received guaranteed income. Most never met a single other participant. This is the room where you do.

Decisions get made without us

Direct cash policy is written in rooms where nobody at the table has received it. A union changes who is in the room and what gets asked before a program is designed.

You get an actual vote

Not an advisory seat, not a feedback survey. Active members hold binding voting rights on what this union does and where it goes.

Skills that stay yours

Training in advocacy, facilitation, running events, and building resources. Whatever else happens, you keep what you learn.

It costs nothing, and it never will

No dues, no fees, no minimum commitment. Show up for what you can, step back when life demands it, and you are still a member.

      About the union

      Our story

      In June of 2025, more than 80 pilot participants came together at the Basic Income Guarantee (BIG) Conference in Washington DC. We took part in a closed session designed by and for pilot participants to share their experiences and talk about their collective power.

      From these conversations, in partnership with Income Movement, an Organizing Committee made exclusively of pilot participants came together in September to envision our future. Coming together weekly, we brainstormed what this group most needed, and documented a bit of what we didn't want to experience, too. Pillars evolved during the visioning process, laying the groundwork for our current work and ethos.

      Inclusion means everyone. Religion, age, race, gender, economic status — everyone is welcomed here.

      Common questions

      Does this cost anything?

      No. There are no dues and no fees. Membership is free.

      Will joining affect my benefits?

      Draft answer — needs review before launch. Joining is free and no money changes hands, so there's nothing to report as income. If you have a question about your own situation, ask your BISC Buddy and we'll help you find someone who can answer it properly.

      Do I have to prove I was in a pilot?

      No. You tell us your pilot and we take your word for it. You've been asked to prove enough.

      What does membership ask of me?

      Nothing required. Come to what you can, take on what interests you, step back when life demands it. You stay a member either way.

      What's an Active Member?

      Draft — pending confirmation from the structures team. Members who take part in three meetings, events, or volunteer tasks within six months become Active Members, which carries binding voting rights in union decisions.

      What is a BISC Buddy?

      A member who's been around a while and checks in with you over your first months. They answer questions, point you toward work that fits, and make sure you're not navigating a new organization alone.

      Who can join right now?

      People who took part in a guaranteed income or basic income pilot, current or past. We're starting there and opening wider as the union grows.

      Questions before you join?

      Ask anything — whether it costs money, whether it affects your benefits, what you'd actually be signing up for. A person answers.
